Project 01 Real Estate & Alternative Investments · University of Amsterdam

Housing types in the Netherlands: segment & regional portfolio optimization

Group project (5 members) · March 2026 · Data: CBS / Kadaster, 1995Q1–2025Q4
Mean-Variance Optimization Amihud Illiquidity Stress Testing Flood-Risk Overlay

We built a mean-variance framework for a Dutch institutional investor choosing between residential housing segments (terraced, corner, semi-detached, detached, apartment) and the twelve provinces plus Amsterdam — then asked whether the standard Markowitz output actually survives contact with illiquidity, a real crisis, and flood risk.

The headline finding: the optimizer wants to concentrate in exactly the provinces most exposed to coastal flooding. That tension, not the optimization itself, is the interesting part.

Key results
GMVP allocation89.2% terraced houses, 10.8% apartments
GMVP vs. passive NL benchmark (Sharpe)1.086 vs. 1.056
Most liquid provinces (Amihud ratio)Zuid-Holland 1.77, Noord-Holland 2.73
2008 crisis: GMVP vs. passive return−2.75% vs. −3.20% ann.
Coastal flood damage, 2050 pessimistic (WRI Aqueduct)6.3% of urban asset value
Methodology
  • Markowitz (1952) mean-variance optimization on quarterly log returns across 7 housing segments and 13 regions, full sample 1995Q2–2025Q4 (123 observations)
  • Amihud (2002) illiquidity ratio, adapted for real estate following Ametefe et al. (2015/2016), using transaction counts in place of trading volume
  • 2008–2013 stress test comparing pre-crisis, crisis, and recovery sub-periods for returns, volatility, transaction volume, and correlation structure
  • Regional flood-risk overlay using the WRI Aqueduct Floods tool (riverine and coastal hazard, 2010–2080 horizons) to assess unmodelled tail risk in the optimal allocation
Project 02 Quantitative Finance & Algorithmic Trading · University of Amsterdam

Macro-adjusted value-momentum strategy with walk-forward analysis

Group 14, 6 members · March 2026 · Backtest: NL / FR / BE equities, July 1998 – May 2022
Factor Investing Walk-Forward Validation Volatility Targeting Backtesting

A market-neutral long/short equity strategy combining Book-to-Market value and 11-month momentum signals, with two macro overlays — a regime tilt based on trailing HML performance, and continuous volatility scaling to a fixed target. Rebalanced monthly, self-financing, no net capital required.

We validated it out-of-sample using a 6-year rolling walk-forward analysis rather than trusting a single in-sample backtest — the whole point being to see whether the strategy would have actually worked if we'd built it a decade ago rather than reverse-engineered it now.

Key results (walk-forward, 1998–2022)
Annualised return / volatility5.28% / 5.69%
Sharpe ratio0.93
Information ratio1.17
CAPM alpha (annualised)5.98%
Max drawdown−9.64%
Win rate80% (20 of 25 years)
Mean walk-forward efficiency1.02
Methodology
  • Signals: Book-to-Market equity (6-month reporting lag, no look-ahead) and 11-month momentum (lagged 1 month to skip short-term reversal), both converted to winsorized cross-sectional z-scores
  • Regime tilt: when the 6-month rolling HML factor return is above its historical median, tilt +10% toward value; otherwise +10% toward momentum (Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen, 2013)
  • Volatility scaling to a fixed annualised target, leverage capped at 2×, following Fan, Li & Liu (2018) and Moreira & Muir (2017)
  • Walk-forward: 6-year optimization window, 2-year trading window, 12 iterations — base value/momentum weights (0.4 / 0.5 / 0.6) re-optimized each cycle by maximizing the Information Ratio, with zero look-ahead in every macro signal
  • Bottom 20% of stocks by market cap excluded before ranking, following Fama & French (2008), to keep the strategy investable
Project 03 Advanced Investments · University of Amsterdam

Replicating Fama-French: size, value, momentum and the five-factor model

Group 4.4, 5 members · Sample: 1965–2023 (708 months), extended through COVID-19
Fama-MacBeth Asset Pricing Momentum Cross-Sectional Regression

We re-ran Fama & French's (1996) 25 size/BE-ME portfolio sort and the Asness-Moskowitz-Pedersen (2013) value-momentum analysis on data through 2023 — nearly 30 years beyond the original studies — to see whether the classic results still hold, or whether they were an artifact of the sample period.

They mostly hold. The more interesting result is what happened to momentum during COVID-19, when the textbook prediction (a momentum crash) didn't materialise.

Key results
Size premium (small vs. big, monthly)+0.208 pp
Value spread (P3−P1, annualised)≈3% alpha, t = 2.14
Momentum spread (P3−P1, annualised)≈6.5% alpha, t = 3.71
Value–momentum correlation−0.23
Best-fitting model (Fama-MacBeth)FF5 — RMW & CMA significant
Momentum during COVID-19 (2019–23)No crash detected (β_B,U insignificant)
Methodology
  • Replicated the Fama-French (1996) 25 size × BE/ME portfolio sort and three-factor time-series regressions on value-weighted NYSE/AMEX/NASDAQ data, 1965–2023
  • Computed long/short correlations between value and momentum portfolios to test the Asness et al. (2013) diversification claim directly, rather than relying on the spread correlation alone
  • Estimated Daniel & Moskowitz (2016) style momentum-crash regressions with bear-market and bear-rebound interaction terms, then re-ran them on the 2019–2023 subsample specifically to test COVID-19
  • Compared CAPM, FF3, and FF5 via Fama-MacBeth cross-sectional regressions on 25 size-investment and size-profitability portfolios
Project 04 KPMG Case Assignment · University of Amsterdam

Valuing ASML: a DCF and trading-multiples cross-check

KPMG-run valuation case · September 2025 · ASML Holding N.V., full three-statement model
DCF Valuation WACC / CAPM Trading Comps Three-Statement Modeling

KPMG ran this as a standalone valuation case: build a full operating model for ASML from public disclosures, forecast it explicitly, and value it two independent ways. I modelled ASML's revenue bottom-up across its seven product lines — EUV, the three DUV lithography types, metrology, and installed-base service — before rolling everything into free cash flow and discounting it back at a CAPM-derived WACC.

The DCF and the trading-comps cross-check didn't agree, and reconciling that gap was the actual point of the exercise: the DCF leans heavily on a 1.3% perpetual growth assumption in the terminal value, while the semiconductor-equipment peer group's multiples had already compressed by the valuation date.

Key results
WACC (CAPM build-up)11.6%
Enterprise value, DCF (31/12/2024)€190.1bn
Shareholder value, DCF€198.1bn
Terminal value as % of enterprise value66.6%
Peer median EV/EBIT (2024A / 2025F)15.4x / 13.6x
Implied shareholder value, trading comps€161.2bn / €143.3bn
Methodology
  • Explicit forecast built bottom-up across ASML's product segments (EUV, ArF immersion, ArF dry, KrF, I-line, Metrology & Inspection, Installed Base Management), each with an independently modelled revenue-growth path through 2031, plus a terminal period
  • WACC built via CAPM: 5% market risk premium, a peer-relevered beta of 1.72, the Dutch 10-year risk-free rate, a sales-weighted country risk premium across ASML's export markets, and an after-tax cost of debt at the 25.8% Dutch statutory rate
  • Terminal value via Gordon growth at 1.3% (long-run Dutch GDP growth), holding the terminal EBITDA margin at 35%
  • Cross-checked against a semiconductor-equipment peer set (Applied Materials, Tokyo Electron, KLA, and Lam Research among those retained after filtering), taking the median EV/EBIT multiple
  • Full three-statement linkage — working capital, PP&E, and tax schedules tie out to the DCF via automated balance checks
Project 05 Master Thesis · University of Amsterdam

What drives virtual asset returns? A three-factor model for CS2 skins

Individual thesis · June 2026 · Supervisor: Dr. Roman Kräussl · Steam Community Market, 2013–2025
Fama-MacBeth Factor Construction Panel Data Characteristics Regression

My Master's thesis asks whether Counter-Strike 2 weapon skins — a multi-billion-dollar market with no cash flows, no dividends, and a 15% transaction fee on every trade — behave like a normal asset class once you build a proper factor model for them. I constructed three Fama-French-style factors from scratch (a case-market factor, a rarity spread, and a liquidity spread) and estimated their risk prices with the Fama-MacBeth two-stage procedure, on more than 1.3 million skin-week observations.

The systematic-risk story turned out to be almost entirely absent — only the liquidity factor is priced, and the three factors together explain barely 2% of return variation. What actually drives skin performance is which item collection a skin belongs to: adding collection fixed effects to the characteristics-stage regression takes its R² from 3.5% to 84.6%.

Key results
Sample1.32M skin-weeks, weapon skins only, 2015w6–2025w52
λ IML — illiquid minus liquid (weekly, Shanken t-stat)+0.43%, t = 3.23
λ MKT / λ HMLInsignificant (t = 0.26 / 0.75)
Three-factor model, avg. R²2.41%
Characteristics-stage R² (attributes → + collection FE)3.50% → 84.55%
StatTrak, characteristics stage−0.28% weekly, t = −8.94
Methodology
  • Weekly panel from the Steam Community Market, cleaned for wash-trading via a rolling-median outlier filter, with flagged weeks re-downloaded at daily frequency
  • Three factors built Fama-French style on weapon skins only: MKT (equal-weighted case-portfolio return), HML (Covert-minus-Mil-Spec rarity spread), IML (illiquid-minus-liquid tercile spread on lagged volume)
  • Two-stage Fama-MacBeth (1973) estimation with Newey-West (1987) standard errors and a Shanken (1992) errors-in-variables correction
  • Characteristics-stage regression of Stage-1 alphas on exterior condition, quality tier, weapon type, StatTrak status, and collection fixed effects
  • Treated the October 2025 Trade-Up Contract rule change as a natural experiment in the platform/supply-side risk unique to virtual-goods markets, where the developer can redefine supply mechanics overnight
Project 06 Bachelor Thesis · University of Amsterdam

Bitcoin as digital gold: diversification across inflation regimes

Group thesis (3 members) · June 2025 · Supervisor: Danjun Xu · US weekly data, May 2015–May 2025
Portfolio Optimization Sharpe-Ratio Testing Robustness Checks Python / Excel

My Bachelor's thesis, with two co-authors, tested Bitcoin's case as "digital gold" directly: does adding it to a traditional US portfolio actually help, and does that hold up separately in low- and high-inflation regimes? We built naive, minimum-variance, and maximum-Sharpe portfolios across equities, corporate bonds, and REITs — with and without Bitcoin — using ten years of weekly data split at the Fed's 2% inflation target.

Bitcoin improved every portfolio type in both regimes, and an equal-Sharpe-ratio test (Jobson-Korkie, Memmel-corrected) confirmed the improvement was statistically significant rather than a lucky sample. Three robustness checks — adding gold, swapping in Chinese assets, and swapping in other cryptocurrencies — mostly held up, with the interesting exception that Litecoin and Dogecoin actually out-diversified Bitcoin on its own.

Key results
Sample526 weekly obs., May 2015–May 2025
Max-Sharpe portfolio, full sample (with vs. without BTC)1.121 vs. 0.370
Equal-Sharpe test, low-inflation regimeΔ = 1.192, p < 0.001
Equal-Sharpe test, high-inflation regimeΔ = 0.130, p < 0.001
BTC weight, max-Sharpe (low vs. high inflation)29.9% vs. 17.5%
Robustness: + gold vs. BTC-only, high-inflation Sharpe0.922 vs. 0.863
Methodology
  • Weekly nominal returns (BTC, S&P 500, Russell 2000, ICE BofA US corporate bond index, FTSE Nareit REIT index) converted to real returns and split into low- (≤2%) and high-inflation (>2%) sub-periods on the Fed's target
  • Three portfolio types per period — naive equal-weight, minimum-variance, and maximum-Sharpe (Markowitz, 1952) — solved by constrained optimization (fully invested, no short positions)
  • Equal-Sharpe-ratio significance test (Jobson & Korkie, 1981; Memmel, 2003 correction), comparing Bitcoin-included and traditional max-Sharpe portfolios in each regime
  • Three robustness checks: gold as a second diversifier, Chinese assets (CSI 300 / CSI 1000, onshore bonds and real estate) replacing the US set, and altcoins (XRP, Litecoin, Dogecoin) alongside and instead of Bitcoin
